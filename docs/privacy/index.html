Privacy

Effective November 6, 2026.

In short:

  • The calculators collect nothing. What you type stays on your device.
  • The newsletter holds what Substack and Stripe collect when you subscribe or pay: your name and email address, which emails you open, any survey answers, and billing details if you pay.
  • Messages you send us are kept, and are read by the publisher and by AI agents that help answer them.

The sections below give the details.

The calculators

Every calculator runs in your own browser, and what you type stays on your device. The calculators use no account, no cookies (small files a site stores in your browser), no browser storage and no analytics. Once the page has loaded, they send nothing to any server. Files you open in a calculator, such as a brokerage statement, are read on your device and never uploaded.

This site

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs each visitor's IP address for security, whether or not they use GitHub. (An IP address is the number that identifies your internet connection.) GitHub's privacy statement covers that log. Apart from it, the site sets no cookies and runs no tracking, and no other company collects information here about what you do on this site or elsewhere.

The newsletter

The newsletter is sent through Substack. When you subscribe, Substack collects your name and email address and gives them to us.

Substack also shows us, for each subscriber, which emails they received and opened and which links they clicked. It adds an activity rating based on things like email opens and web views.

The sign-up survey is optional. It asks how you're paid and what you'd most like to read about. Your answers are linked to your email address, and we use them only to choose topics.

If you buy a paid subscription on the web, Stripe processes the payment. Through Stripe we can see your billing details, but never your full card number. If you pay through Apple in Substack's iPhone app, Apple handles the payment, and we see your email address but no payment details.

Other companies on Substack's pages

Substack's own pages, where you subscribe, read and pay, work differently from this site, and other companies may collect information about you there. Substack says it uses cookies, some set by third parties, and may collect information about your activity after you leave its site. We haven't added any analytics or advertising trackers to our Substack pages. Substack's privacy policy and Stripe's cover what they collect.

Email you send us

The publisher reads your message. AI agents that help run the newsletter also read it and draft replies, so its text goes through Anthropic's Claude service. Depending on the publisher's account settings, Anthropic may keep that text and use it to improve its models; Anthropic's privacy policy explains how.

Please don't send account numbers, tax ID numbers or your own figures.

We keep messages in the publisher's email and a private folder, use them only to answer you and fix errors, and keep them until you ask us to delete them. With your permission, and with anything that identifies you removed, a question may become the question in an issue. Your name never appears in public without your written permission.

Who gets your information

Only the services that run the newsletter and this site: Substack (sign-up and email), Stripe or Apple (payments), GitHub (this site), the publisher's email provider, and Anthropic (help answering mail). We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't share it with sponsors. The calculators never send us your income, holdings or tax details.

Your choices

  • Unsubscribe with the link at the foot of every email, or in your Substack settings.
  • See, correct or delete what we hold by writing to andzrev@pm.me or replying to any issue. We'll answer within 30 days. You can also delete your Substack account in your Substack settings.
  • Do Not Track is a browser setting that asks sites not to follow you around the web. Beyond GitHub's security log, this site and the calculators track no one, so there is nothing for the setting to switch off. Substack says its services don't respond to it.

Changes

A changed policy goes on this page with a new effective date. The next issue mentions any material change.
