# Stock pay tax cheat sheet, tax year 2026

*How restricted stock units (RSUs), employee stock purchase plans (ESPPs) and stock options are taxed. Federal rules; states differ. General information, not tax, investment or legal advice. Researched, written and checked by AI agents from the sources below; not reviewed by an accountant or lawyer.*

## When each is taxed

| Kind | At grant | At vesting, purchase or exercise | At sale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RSUs | Nothing | Shares' value is W-2 wages | Gain or loss above the taxed value |
| ESPP (Section 423, the tax-favored kind) | Nothing | Nothing | Part wages, part capital gain |
| Incentive stock options (ISOs) | Nothing | No regular tax; the spread counts for the alternative minimum tax (AMT), a second way of figuring tax that can add to the bill | Capital gain if held long enough |
| Nonqualified options (NSOs) | Usually nothing | The spread is W-2 wages | Gain or loss above the taxed value |

The spread is the share's value that day minus the price paid.

## 2026 numbers

- **Withholding on stock pay:** stock and bonuses are supplemental pay, pay outside the regular salary, often withheld at a flat 22%; 37% on supplemental pay over $1 million a year. The top tax rate is 37%, so withholding can fall short.
- **Social Security:** 6.2% of wages up to $184,500. **Medicare:** 1.45%, plus 0.9% on wages over $250,000 joint, $125,000 married filing separately, $200,000 otherwise; employers withhold it past $200,000.
- **AMT exemption:** $90,100 single or head of household, $140,200 joint, $70,100 married filing separately. It shrinks 50 cents per dollar of income figured for the AMT over $1,000,000 joint, $500,000 for everyone else.
- **Long-term capital gain:** held more than one year.

## ESPP and ISO holding periods

A qualifying sale comes after the later of 2 years from grant (an ESPP's offering date) and 1 year from purchase or exercise. Your plan's documents set the dates, the discount and any lookback (a price based on the lower of the offering-date and purchase-date values).

- **ESPP, qualifying:** wages are the lesser of the actual gain and the grant-date discount (in a 15% plan, 15% of the grant-date value); zero after a loss.
- **ESPP, sold sooner:** wages are the purchase-date value minus the price paid.
- **ISO, sold sooner:** wages are the spread at exercise, capped at the gain; zero after a loss.

No Social Security, Medicare or income tax is withheld on these wages.

## Cost basis

Basis is what the shares count as costing for tax: the price paid plus what was taxed as wages. For stock granted or acquired after 2013, the broker's Form 1099-B can't include the wage part, so its basis may be too low. Form 8949 corrects it (code B).

## Section 83(b)

For restricted stock (shares you own but can lose until they vest), including options exercised before vesting, an 83(b) election taxes the value now, not at vesting. It's due within 30 days of the transfer, on Form 15620 or a signed written statement, and can be revoked only with IRS consent, which is given for a mistake of fact, not a fall in the stock's value. RSUs and options themselves don't qualify.
