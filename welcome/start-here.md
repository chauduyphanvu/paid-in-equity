# Start here

*What Paid in Equity is, the four people it follows, and where everything lives*

Paid in Equity answers one money question every Tuesday for people paid partly in company stock: restricted stock units (RSUs), an employee stock purchase plan (ESPP) or stock options. It's for engineers, product managers, designers, recruiters, salespeople and their managers.

Each answer shows its arithmetic, and most come with a free calculator that runs in your browser. Nothing you type leaves the page.

## The four people every issue follows

These four are fictional, and their pay and grants stay the same all year.

- **Priya**, 24, a new-graduate engineer at a public company in Washington, earning $180,000 with RSUs and an ESPP
- **Marcus**, a senior product manager in California earning $350,000, with RSUs and an ESPP, married filing jointly
- **Dana**, a staff engineer at a late-stage startup in California, paid partly in incentive stock options
- **Leo**, an enterprise sales lead in Texas earning $280,000 in salary, commissions and RSUs, filing as head of household

Each issue leads with whichever one the question suits; on the calculator site, a switch shows all four.

## The calculators

All of them live on the [calculator site]({{site_url}}), free and with no sign-up. The first, a withholding gap calculator, which compares the tax taken from pay with the tax the stock adds, arrives with the first issue on November 17. This list grows each week.

## How it's made, and how errors are fixed

AI agents research, write and check each issue. Every figure comes from a primary source, such as an IRS publication or a form's instructions, and the calculators are tested against the IRS's own worked examples wherever the IRS publishes one. The code is public on [GitHub]({{github_url}}).

If you find a mistake, reply to any issue. Confirmed errors get a dated correction on the post, a line in the next email, and an entry in the public [corrections log]({{site_url}}/corrections/).

It's general information worked through fictional examples, not tax, investment or legal advice for your situation ([disclaimer]({{site_url}}/disclaimer/), [privacy]({{site_url}}/privacy/)).
